April 2020

The week that decided what I would build for.

I was a contractor engineer inside the Small Business Administration when the Paycheck Protection Program launched. It was the emergency lifeline for American small businesses: forgivable loans that decided whether a restaurant, a shop, or a two person agency survived the year. The systems were not built for it.

Apr 3Paycheck Protection Program opens. A nation of small businesses arrives at once.
Apr 6E-Tran, the loan portal, goes down. Unreachable for up to four hours.
Apr 6Every hour it is down, owners cannot reach the money keeping their people paid.
Apr 8A new entry point into the loan system ships. Built with AWS.
Apr 8381,000 loans · $100 billion · 3,600 lenders, by that afternoon.
Apr 16Funding exhausted. More than one million small businesses approved.

The SBA normally backs about $25 billion a year in 7(a) loans. PPP asked the same infrastructure to move that in days, roughly fifty times normal capacity. At that volume the hard part is never the feature. It is consistency under load, recovery you can trust, and designing for the failure rather than the demo, because a partial write is somebody's payroll.

Every one of those millions of clicks was an owner trying to keep their business alive. That is the same person I build for now.

Timeline corroborated by contemporaneous reporting, including American Banker, "After opening-day fiasco, SBA upgrades lender portal with Amazon assist."
